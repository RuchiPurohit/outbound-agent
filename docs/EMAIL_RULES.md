# Email Rules

## First-touch content

- Normally stay under 100 words, including the signature.
- Write as a technical founder or engineer speaking to another engineer.
- Use a short, specific subject. No clickbait or fabricated familiarity.
- Reference exactly one verified prospect research signal in the email body.
- Explain a plausible technical pain hypothesis using conditional wording:
  "if", "may", "could", or "as this grows". Never claim an unverified problem.
- Explain how ConvoKit's chat infrastructure relates without inventing product
  capabilities, customer names, performance numbers, or proof points.
- End with one low-friction CTA, such as offering the API docs.
- Sign as Ruchi. Do not invent previous contact or a personal relationship.
- Avoid generic company descriptions, bulk-outreach language, and sales hype.
- Do not use: "Hope you're doing well", "game changer", "revolutionary",
  "touch base", "circle back", or "unlock value".

## Structure

1. A specific, sourced launch, technical post, hiring signal, or relevant change.
2. The plausible infrastructure problem it may create.
3. How ConvoKit relates.
4. A short question / low-friction CTA.

## Evidence and review

Store the signal's research ID with the draft so the dashboard can display its
source beside the email. A public email listing is not a personalization signal.
If there is no credible signal, skip drafting rather than inventing one.

Every generated or rewritten email stays `DRAFT`. Only an explicit human
approval can advance it to `READY_TO_SEND`. Rejected drafts stay `REJECTED`.
Never send messages or create Gmail drafts during generation or rewriting.

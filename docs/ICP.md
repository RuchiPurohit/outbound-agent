# Conversion-oriented ICP

Product:
ConvoKit

What it does:
Hosted chat infrastructure that developers integrate when human-to-human chat
is useful to their product but building and operating messaging is not their
core business.

## Commercial objective

The objective is to find leads likely to adopt ConvoKit, not merely companies
whose products resemble existing chat customers.

The best lead has a live human-to-human product workflow, a credible reason to
add messaging, a team capable of integrating it, and—once contact discovery is
authorized—a reachable decision maker. Embedded chat does not need to exist
today. A sourced product workflow plus a specific chat hypothesis is sufficient
to qualify at the company-discovery stage.

Do not assume a company with existing chat is a strong lead. Replacing working
infrastructure creates switching cost. Existing-chat companies rank below
greenfield opportunities unless public evidence shows a current reason to
replace, rebuild, expand, or reconsider the implementation.

## Ideal customer profile

### Primary: greenfield product teams

Prioritize live products where two or more human users already interact around
a shared activity or object, but no embedded human-to-human chat is publicly
evident. Strong examples include:

- multiplayer and social games, including simple board, card, trivia, fantasy,
  tournament, and casual games
- two-sided marketplaces and on-demand or gig applications
- dating, friendship, community, club, and membership applications
- education, tutoring, cohort, coaching, fitness, habit, and accountability apps
- telehealth and therapy platforms with a verified human care workflow
- property, rental, coliving, roommate, and local-service products
- collaborative B2B SaaS, project, client, creator, and professional workflows
- products introducing multiplayer, social, community, collaboration, group,
  marketplace, booking, matching, or user-generated-content features

A simple multiplayer Ludo app can qualify when real human players share a match
or social graph and could plausibly use match chat, reactions, invitations,
rematches, clubs, or tournament coordination. The hypothesis must be anchored
to a sourced feature or audience; do not claim the company plans to build chat.

### Primary: teams with a current build trigger

Rank especially highly when a recent source shows:

- a launch or roadmap direction involving multiplayer, groups, community,
  collaboration, matching, bookings, marketplaces, or creator/user interaction
- hiring for product, mobile, backend, platform, realtime, community, growth, or
  engagement work connected to the qualifying workflow
- a public request to build chat or realtime communication
- users coordinating through email, WhatsApp, Discord, Slack, comments, support,
  or another external channel that could move into the product
- new funding, an accelerator batch, active product expansion, or a new platform
  that creates a credible implementation window

### Primary: channel partners

Mobile, Flutter, React Native, product-development, and low-code agencies can
introduce ConvoKit into multiple customer projects. Qualify the agency using a
sourced portfolio in relevant product categories and evidence of supported
delivery technology. Do not invent project volume, partner economics, or a
formal partner program.

### Secondary: existing-chat products with a switching trigger

Persist an existing-chat company as a high-priority lead only when a source
supports at least one of:

- dissatisfaction with its current vendor or implementation
- a public reliability, scalability, moderation, customization, analytics, or
  integration limitation relevant to confirmed ConvoKit capabilities
- an active rebuild, redesign, migration, platform expansion, or vendor review
- communication still occurring outside the product despite embedded chat
- a limited chat surface that a newly launched workflow plausibly needs to extend

Existing chat without a switching or expansion trigger is valid product-fit
evidence but weak conversion evidence. Mature messaging infrastructure that is
core IP is a negative, not an attractive replacement target.

## Company and buyer profile

Default geography:

- United States
- Canada

An explicit campaign segment may override geography.

Preferred company shape:

- founder-led or a small product/engineering organization likely to have a short
  decision path; confirm the actual buyer only during contact discovery
- roughly 2–100 employees, with strongest preference for 5–50
- pre-seed through Series A, or a similarly small bootstrapped company with a
  live product and evidence of active development
- enough product maturity to integrate infrastructure, but without enterprise
  procurement or a dedicated messaging-platform organization

Companies up to 200 employees may qualify when the buyer and trigger are strong.
Unknown headcount or funding does not automatically disqualify an otherwise
convincing product and buyer opportunity. Engineering headcount is `UNKNOWN`
unless a public source states it; never infer it from total headcount.

Preferred buyers, in order:

1. Technical founder, founder/CTO, or hands-on founder
2. CTO, VP Engineering, or Head of Engineering
3. Product-oriented engineering leader responsible for mobile, backend,
   platform, realtime, growth, or the qualifying workflow
4. Staff or Principal Engineer with clear ownership of the relevant system
5. Engineering Manager with clear ownership of the relevant system

Conversion requires someone who can plausibly decide or champion an
integration. A sourced, reachable founder at a small company can be more useful
than a perfect title with no visible responsibility. Contact confidence remains
separate from company fit and is evaluated only after the human approves the
company for contact discovery.

## Product fit

The prospect should want chat as a feature, not sell chat as its product. The
team should benefit from owning or customizing its UI while outsourcing
conversations, participants, message storage, access, realtime delivery, and
operations.

Confirmed ConvoKit capabilities are defined only in `docs/PRODUCT.md`. Current
confirmed surfaces include Flutter, React web, and React Native, with custom UI,
webhooks, participant access, realtime delivery, and audit trails. Do not claim
unverified compliance, moderation, scale, pricing, migration tooling, service
levels, or vendor-comparison advantages.

Technology fit helps conversion but is not mandatory when the public stack is
unknown. Never reject a strong workflow merely because its implementation stack
is not public.

## Messaging-opportunity qualification gate

A company qualifies only when public evidence establishes a real product,
audience, or workflow and supports one of these paths:

1. **Greenfield human messaging opportunity — preferred.** Two human roles, two
   players, or a human group share a sourced activity or product object and have
   a concrete reason to communicate. Chat need not exist.
2. **Product-specific engagement opportunity — preferred.** A sourced feature
   or audience makes chat, reactions, coordination, invitations, groups, or
   conversation a plausible engagement improvement, even when both participant
   roles are not explicitly documented.
3. **External or fragmented communication — strong.** Users currently coordinate
   through an external channel, comments, forums, support discussions, email, or
   manual workflows connected to the product.
4. **Existing embedded messaging — conditional.** Human-to-human chat exists and
   there is a sourced switching, rebuilding, limitation, or expansion trigger.
   Existing chat alone does not qualify a company. Without that trigger, it must
   independently qualify through another product-specific opportunity and
   should rank low.

Every persisted company needs a thesis with two separate clauses:

> Observed: [specific sourced product, audience, or human workflow]; hypothesis:
> ConvoKit may help [specific participants communicate or coordinate in a named
> product context].

The observation is verified; the possible benefit is explicitly hypothetical.
`NO_PUBLIC_EVIDENCE` means a completed public search found no reliable evidence
of chat. It is not proof of absence. `UNKNOWN` means the check was incomplete.

Human-to-AI chat alone is not a ConvoKit use case. An AI product may still
qualify through a separate human-to-human workflow or product-specific social
opportunity.

## Conversion-priority tiers

### Tier 1 — pursue first

- strong greenfield human communication opportunity
- live product or credible near-launch product
- recent implementation or product-expansion trigger
- small sourced team or stage indicating a short likely decision path
- supported stack is known, or stack is unknown but the other signals are strong

### Tier 2 — pursue selectively

- strong greenfield workflow but no dated trigger
- good agency/channel-partner fit with a relevant sourced portfolio
- external communication that could plausibly move in-product
- existing chat with a sourced limitation, rebuild, migration, or expansion need

### Tier 3 — research or nurture

- plausible chat hypothesis but weak proof of active usage or team readiness
- existing chat with no switching trigger
- product is early, inactive, or has no current implementation window

Tier 3 may be persisted for human review, but should not displace Tier 1 or Tier
2 leads merely to reach a requested count.

## ICP scoring rubric (100 points)

Apply the qualification gate first. Use the existing six stored score fields so
historic campaigns and the SQLite schema remain compatible. Unknown or
unsupported evidence earns zero. Do not double-count one source as proof of
unrelated factors.

| Stored factor | Points | Conversion-oriented interpretation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Messaging workflow fit | 0–30 | Strength and specificity of the human workflow or product-specific engagement opportunity |
| Chat implementation opportunity | 0–20 | Greenfield build opportunity, external/fragmented communication, or a sourced switching/expansion trigger |
| Timely buying signal | 0–15 | Recent evidence that creates an implementation window now |
| Team/stage fit | 0–15 | Small, capable team and stage suggesting a short decision path |
| Supported stack fit | 0–10 | Sourced Flutter, React web, or React Native product surface |
| Live-product evidence | 0–10 | Evidence that the product is usable, launched, or actively reaching real users |

### Scoring calibration

`workflowFit`:

- 0: no sourced product workflow or audience
- 5–10: generic social possibility with little product-specific detail
- 11–20: sourced shared activity or audience with a concrete chat hypothesis
- 21–26: named human roles or players communicating around a specific match,
  booking, listing, project, session, order, group, or other product object
- 27–30: especially frequent, consequential, or central coordination need

Existing chat is not required for a high `workflowFit` score.

`chatImplementation`:

- 0–3: implementation state is unknown and the opportunity is generic
- 4–8: no public chat evidence after a completed search, with a credible and
  product-specific greenfield hypothesis
- 9–14: strong greenfield opportunity, external communication, or an apparent
  gap anchored to a sourced workflow
- 15–20: explicit chat build request, external-channel pain, planned messaging,
  or a sourced reason to replace, rebuild, or expand an existing implementation

`NONE_FOUND` alone earns no points, but `NONE_FOUND` plus a strong sourced
greenfield workflow can earn substantial points. Existing chat alone earns no
implementation-opportunity premium.

`timingSignal`:

- 0: no dated reason to act
- 3–6: recent funding, accelerator participation, general product hiring, or a
  relevant launch without direct communication scope
- 7–11: recent social, multiplayer, marketplace, collaboration, community, or
  platform expansion connected to the hypothesis
- 12–15: explicit messaging work, a build request, an active rebuild/migration,
  or attributable dissatisfaction with the current approach

`teamFit`:

- 0–4: team readiness, size, and stage are unclear or poorly aligned
- 5–9: either company size or stage is sourced and reasonably aligned
- 10–13: small product team and early stage are both sourced and aligned
- 14–15: live, founder-led or similarly compact product team with strong sourced
  evidence of a short likely decision path

Use source-stated headcount or range where available. A strong small-team signal
may still score when formal funding is unknown.

`stackFit` and `liveProduct` use their table definitions. A supported stack can
reduce integration friction but cannot rescue a weak human workflow. A live
product matters because a hypothetical use case without users or active product
development is less likely to convert.

### Ranking rules

- Rank conversion opportunity, not sophistication of the current chat system.
- Prefer a 70-point greenfield lead over a 75-point existing-chat lead when the
  latter has no switching trigger; explain the ordering in the report.
- As a default company-approval threshold, prioritize scores of 60 or higher
  with at least 10 `chatImplementation` points and a dated trigger. A strong
  greenfield company without a dated trigger may remain Tier 2 for human review.
- Never award points because a claim merely seems likely.
- Explain borderline scores and missing conversion evidence in the report.

## Research channels

Prioritize sources likely to reveal an implementation window:

- recent Y Combinator and other credible accelerator batches
- Product Hunt and public launch posts for multiplayer, marketplace, community,
  social, collaborative, creator, education, fitness, and service products
- app-store listings, official help centers, changelogs, roadmaps, and product
  documentation showing participant workflows
- public engineering or product jobs tied to realtime, mobile, backend,
  community, engagement, marketplace, multiplayer, or collaboration work
- attributable GitHub, Reddit, Indie Hackers, Flutter, React Native, and founder
  posts requesting chat or describing a messaging/vendor problem
- relevant mobile and product agencies with sourced client portfolios

Jobs and community posts are leads, not automatic proof. Verify company
identity, domain, affiliation, date, and product fit before persistence. Never
contact a poster during research. Prefer first-party evidence for qualification.

## Negative and exclusion signals

Deprioritize:

- existing embedded chat with no sourced switching or expansion trigger
- organizational scale or structure suggests a long decision path
- no evidence of a live or actively developed product
- enterprise-scale procurement where ConvoKit has no verified advantage
- general agencies without relevant portfolio or technology evidence
- a communication idea that is generic rather than tied to the product

Exclude:

- single-player or bot-only products with no separate human interaction path
- human-to-AI assistants, copilots, or chatbots without a distinct human-to-human
  workflow or sourced social opportunity
- products selling messaging, community infrastructure, or realtime
  collaboration as their mature core product/IP
- companies whose use case requires unverified ConvoKit capabilities
- abandoned products or companies whose identity cannot be verified

Apply the core-IP test by inspecting the homepage headline and primary pricing
page. A marketplace, multiplayer game, community app, or collaborative product
is not excluded merely because communication would be important; exclude only
when building/selling messaging or realtime collaboration itself is the core
offering and no distinct workflow remains for ConvoKit.

## Research and outreach discipline

- Important claims require a public source URL.
- Never invent chat plans, pain, employee counts, funding, stack, titles, or
  switching intent.
- Keep observed facts separate from hypotheses.
- Company fit, buyer confidence, and email availability are separate judgments.
- Company discovery must not research or persist contacts. Buyer reachability is
  evaluated only for companies the human approves for contact discovery.
- Do not reject a strong company solely because a perfect technical title is not
  verified; persist clearly labeled fallback contacts for human review.
- Store at most two contacts per company.
- Human review determines which companies and contacts proceed.
- Every first-touch email still requires explicit approval.
